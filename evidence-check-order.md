# Evidence Check Order for a Tentative Photo-Based Rock Identification

1. **Describe the fabric first.** Record whether the specimen looks granular, layered, glassy, vesicular, foliated, or crystalline.
2. **Describe luster and transparency.** Note what the photographed surface does in light; do not turn one bright spot into a whole-specimen property.
3. **Describe existing breaks in context.** Record repeated flat, irregular, or curved edges without making a fresh break.
4. **Add discovery setting.** Write where the specimen was found and whether it was loose, attached to outcrop, or mixed with imported material.
5. **Keep a shortlist.** If a tentative name conflicts with visible observations, retain up to three alternatives and name one safe observation that could separate them.
6. **Preserve uncertainty.** A photograph cannot measure hardness, density, chemistry, internal structure, age, provenance, or value. Undetermined from this photograph is a valid field note.

## Stop and seek in-person help when it matters

Do not use a photograph to confirm a meteorite, appraise a gem, decide whether a powder or fibrous material is safe, or make a collecting decision governed by access or regulations. A museum, geological survey, university geology department, or qualified rock and mineral club may be able to examine a specimen in person.
